Cloud Vulnerabilities and Risk Mitigation
1. Common Cloud Vulnerabilities
Vulnerability Description
Data Breaches Unauthorized access to sensitive data due to misconfigured storage,
weak access controls, or poor encryption. Example: Publicly accessible
object storage exposing customer records.
Insecure APIs Weak authentication/authorization or improper input validation in
management APIs can be exploited for data exposure and privilege
escalation.
Misconfigurations Human errors (overly permissive buckets, open security groups, weak
firewall rules) often lead to accidental exposure.
Account Hijacking Compromised credentials (phishing, brute force, token theft) enable
attackers to abuse resources or exfiltrate data.
DoS/DDoS Volumetric or application-layer attacks overwhelm services, causing
downtime and unexpected scaling costs.
Insider Threats Malicious or negligent insiders misuse legitimate access; hard to detect
without strong monitoring and least privilege.
Shared Technology Risks Multi-tenant infrastructure and hypervisor flaws may enable
cross-tenant attacks if not patched and isolated properly.
Compliance/Legal Risks Data residency and regulatory requirements (GDPR, HIPAA) may be
violated due to improper region selection or lack of controls.

2. Risk Mitigation Strategies
Control Area Practices
Data Security Encrypt data in transit and at rest; manage keys with KMS/HSM; apply
tokenization and anonymization for sensitive fields.
Identity & Access Management Enforce least privilege; MFA for all users; shortnlived credentials;
(IAM) RBAC/ABAC; periodic access reviews; deny-by-default.
Secure APIs Use OAuth 2.0/JWT; validate inputs and outputs; apply rate limiting and
WAF; log and monitor all API calls.
Configuration Management Continuous posture management (AWS Config, Azure Defender, GCP
SCC); vulnerability scans; IaC with policy-as-code and
prendeployment checks.
Threat Detection & Monitoring Centralized logging and SIEM; anomaly detection; alerting on highnrisk
events; EDR for workloads.
Resilience to DoS/DDoS CDN and Anycast; autoscaling; managed DDoS protection; WAF rules
for L7; rate limiting and surge protection.
Vendor & Shared Responsibility Select certified providers (ISO 27001, SOC 2, FedRAMP); understand
shared responsibilities; review SLAs and audit reports.
Training & Awareness Regular security training; phishing simulations; secure coding
programs; incident response drills.
3. Summary
Cloud platforms introduce unique risks, but a layered defense—encryption, strong IAM, secure APIs,
continuous monitoring, configuration governance, DDoS resilience, vendor diligence, and staff
training—reduces exposure and supports compliance. Align controls to the shared responsibility model and
your regulatory context.
